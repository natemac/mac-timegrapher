GUIDE — SIMPLIFIED DRAFT

================================================================
1. MEASURE OR INSPECTION
================================================================
Two jobs. Pick one before you start.

Measure — one live reading that updates as you watch. Use this
while adjusting the watch. Nothing is saved unless you press
Capture, which saves the screen as an image.

Inspection — takes the watch through six positions, records
each, and produces a document you can print or hand over.


================================================================
2. AUDIO INPUT
================================================================
Which microphone is listening.

A watch is far too quiet for a normal microphone. You need a
contact sensor — a USB timegrapher pickup, or a phone resting
against the watch. A laptop's built-in mic mostly hears the room.

Pick your device from the list. The number beside it is how fast
it is sampling; higher is better, and anything the app accepts
will work.


================================================================
3. THE FOUR READINGS
================================================================

RATE — seconds per day
  How much the watch gains or loses in 24 hours.
  Positive is fast, negative is slow.
  This is the one you adjust.

    within ±10 s/day    fine for most watches
    −4 to +6 s/day      chronometer grade

AMPLITUDE — degrees
  How far the balance wheel swings. The balance wheel is the
  part that rocks back and forth; it is what makes the ticking.
  This tells you the health of the movement, not its accuracy.

    270–310°     healthy, wound, lying flat
    below 250°   old oil, dirt, or a tired mainspring

  It normally drops when the watch is upright, and as it
  unwinds. That is expected.

BEAT ERROR — milliseconds
  Whether the tick and the tock are evenly spaced. Like a limp:
  it still runs, just unevenly.

    under 0.5 ms   good
    under 0.3 ms   very good

  Fixing this is a different adjustment from rate.

BEAT RATE — beats per hour
  How fast the movement is designed to tick. Not a fault.
  Common values are 21,600 and 28,800.
  If it shows something odd, the app is probably hearing
  something other than the watch.

THE SMALL ± UNDER EACH NUMBER
  How much that reading has been moving. A wide spread means
  it has not settled. Do not write it down yet.


================================================================
4. SETTLING
================================================================
Whether the reading has stopped moving enough to trust.

The dot drifts while the reading is still finding itself, then
draws in and turns green.

Wait for Settled before recording anything.

Not settling is usually one of three things:
  - the watch is not held firmly against the sensor
  - the room is too noisy
  - the watch genuinely is not running steadily


================================================================
5. SIGNAL
================================================================
How clearly the ticking stands out from background noise.

  Good / Excellent   you can trust the readings
  Weak / Fair        press the watch harder against the sensor,
                     or move somewhere quieter
  Too loud           the sound is distorting

Too loud matters more than it sounds. Amplitude is worked out
from the shape of each tick, and a distorted tick is the wrong
shape. Slightly quiet and clean beats loud and distorted.

On a computer you can turn the input level down in your system
sound settings. On a phone or tablet you cannot, so move the
sensor instead.


================================================================
6. TRACE
================================================================
The classic scrolling strip. Two lines: tick and tock.

  straight down      keeping time
  leaning right      gaining
  leaning left       losing
  steeper            further off
  gap between lines  the beat error
  fuzzy or scattered dirty movement, or poor contact

Adjust the watch and the slope changes immediately — before
the numbers catch up. That is what this view is for.

Lines that run off one edge reappear on the other. If they wrap
too fast to read, choose a wider magnification in Settings.


================================================================
7. BEAT
================================================================
One tick, magnified. This is the sound the readings come from.

A healthy tick is two or three sharp spikes close together.
Tick and tock should look alike.

  one weaker or smeared     the two sides are not doing
                            equal work
  spread-out middle spike   worn or dirty parts
  extra spikes              the balance is swinging too far

The green line marks the part amplitude is measured from. Read
up to the top scale from there and you get the same amplitude
the readings show, as a position instead of a number.


================================================================
8. WAVEFORM
================================================================
The raw sound, for checking the sensor is working.

  evenly spaced spikes in pairs, quiet between   correct
  continuous fuzz with no pattern                hearing the
                                                 room, not
                                                 the watch

If it is fuzz, fix the contact before suspecting anything else.


================================================================
9. RUNNING AN INSPECTION
================================================================
One reading tells you the watch is fast. Six tell you why.

A watch that reads well in one position and badly in another
has a mechanical problem. One that reads the same everywhere
just needs adjusting. Six positions separate those two cases.

The loop:
  1. The panel names a position. Put the watch that way on
     the sensor.
  2. Press Start. It counts down three seconds — that is for
     you to let go, because your hand is a noise too.
  3. It listens, and records once the reading settles.
  4. It stops on its own and names the next position.

With Auto on, that is the whole job. Turn, press Start, wait.
Turn Auto off and you decide when to record.

If a position will not settle after a minute, the app says so
and lets you record it anyway.


================================================================
10. USB PICKUPS ON ANDROID
================================================================
Only relevant on an Android phone or tablet.

Android will not let a browser choose a microphone unless the
browser also turns on echo cancellation. The app does this for
you, automatically, and only on Android.

On Chrome and browsers built on it, that path adds automatic
volume control that cannot be switched off. Rate and beat error
are unaffected. Amplitude is not reliable, and the app says so
underneath the number.

Firefox on Android does not have this problem.


================================================================
SETTINGS
================================================================

MOVEMENT
  Which watch movement is on the sensor.

  The app works out the beat rate by itself. It cannot work out
  the amount the balance wheel swings per tick — that depends on
  the movement's design — and amplitude is calculated from it.
  Pick the wrong movement and amplitude will be wrong while
  looking perfectly normal.

  Quartz movements are listed so an inspection can name them.
  They have no balance wheel, so amplitude and beat error do not
  apply and are left blank.

TRACE MAGNIFICATION
  How much the trace exaggerates the slope. Smaller numbers
  magnify more. Auto is usually right.

TRACE HISTORY
  How far back the trace remembers. Longer shows the trend;
  shorter reacts faster while you adjust.

BRANDING
  Whether the MAC logo appears in the app and on documents.
  Off unless you turn it on.

SESSION DIAGNOSTICS
  Exports a text file describing what the last run did — every
  reading, the signal level, and the equipment settings.

  For sending to someone when a reading looks wrong. It stays on
  your device until you export it. It names your audio device
  and browser, so read it before sending it on.

STEADINESS OF THIS BENCH
  Not a setting. It shows the steadiest each reading has been
  this session, beside the target it has to beat to count as
  settled.

  Put a known-good, fully wound watch on the sensor, let it run
  a minute, and read the left column. Comfortably under the
  target means your setup is good. Never getting there usually
  means the sensor is not held firmly enough.

QUARTZ CALIBRATION
  Sound cards do not run at exactly the speed they claim, and
  the difference shifts every rate reading by the same amount.
  It is invisible otherwise — the reading looks perfectly steady
  and is simply wrong by a fixed amount.

  To correct it, measure a quartz watch, which keeps far better
  time than any sound card. The app compares what it hears
  against what it should hear and works out the difference.

  You can also type a correction in by hand if you measured it
  elsewhere.

  A correction is only as good as the quartz watch you used. It
  belongs to the audio device, so measure it again if you change
  sensor or computer.

DEVICE CHECK
  Two parts.

  A live list of checks — microphone, sample rate, timing,
  and whether the browser is interfering with the sound.
  Green means ready.

  And a full test, about ninety seconds, which tries every
  combination of audio settings and reports which one works.
  Use it when the app cannot hear the watch at all.


================================================================
WHAT WAS CUT, AND WHY
================================================================

Removed as out of date:
  - "System clock" calibration. The guide still describes it as
    one of two ways to measure the audio clock. That feature was
    removed; only the quartz method exists now.

Removed as too much detail for a first-time user:
  - Parts-per-million arithmetic behind clock error.
  - Named watch parts in the Beat section (pallet stones,
    escape wheel, rebanking).
  - The reasoning for why settled thresholds cannot be chosen
    in advance.
  - The explanation of why branding defaults to off.
  - Note that a correction matches native tg's "cal" value.
  - "Positional spread" as a term.

Kept, deliberately:
  - Every number that tells you good from bad.
  - Every instruction that changes what you should do next.
  - The Android amplitude warning, because a wrong number that
    looks right is worse than no number.

Roughly 3,300 words down to roughly 1,300.
